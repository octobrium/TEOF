# Operational Patterns

Patterns about how to operate effectively. Practical application of TEOF principles.

---

## Reality Contact Required

- **H-Level:** H5-H6 (Intelligence, Truth)
- **Domain:** operational
- **Statement:** All derivations require periodic reality contact. Thought without contact accumulates drift. Feedback from actual outcomes is the only correction mechanism. The longer without contact, the greater the accumulated error.
- **Key insight:** You can reason perfectly from false premises. Only reality contact catches premise errors.
- **Application:** Test predictions. Measure outcomes. Update on evidence. Maintain feedback loops.

---

## Knowing vs Doing Gap

- **H-Level:** H5 (Intelligence)
- **Domain:** operational, behavioral
- **Statement:** Knowing what to do and doing it are different operations with different bottlenecks. The gap between them is where frameworks fail. Information alone doesn't change behavior. Knowledge must be converted into action through practice, systems, and environment design.
- **Key insight:** If you know what to do but don't do it, the problem isn't knowledge — it's the conversion mechanism.
- **Application:** Don't just learn; install. Create systems that make correct action the default path.

---

## Direction is Forward

- **H-Level:** H3 (Direction)
- **Domain:** operational
- **Statement:** Mistakes, failures, and sub-optimal choices are normal parts of any trajectory. The relevant question is not "did I fail?" but "am I moving in the right direction?" Course correction happens through feedback, not through avoiding errors. Progress is iterative, not linear.
- **Key insight:** A system that never fails isn't learning. A system that can't course-correct can't persist.
- **Application:** Optimize for direction and correction speed, not for avoiding all errors.

## Player vs Character Orientation

- **H-Level:** H9-H10 (Freedom, Meaning)
- **Domain:** operational, behavioral
- **Statement:** Two orientations to existence: Character (subject to the game, defined by its metrics, at mercy of outcomes) vs Player (chose to enter, plays by metrics voluntarily, observes the game as game). The Character is trapped. The Player is free while playing the same game.
- **Key insight:** The difference is not circumstance but perspective. Recognition transforms relationship to circumstances.
- **Application:** When feeling trapped, ask: Am I a character who must play, or a player who chose to play?

## Drift and Reset Cycle

- **H-Level:** H4-H5 (Defense, Intelligence)
- **Domain:** operational
- **Statement:** Systems naturally drift from optimal state. Rather than fighting drift constantly, design reset mechanisms that periodically restore proper configuration. Accept drift as normal. Build in resets. Use triggers, not continuous monitoring.
- **Key insight:** Continuous maintenance is expensive. Periodic resets are cheaper and more robust.
- **Application:** Instead of constant vigilance, design triggers that detect when reset is needed and procedures that execute it.

---

## Propose Don't Decide

- **H-Level:** H5-H7 (Intelligence, Truth, Power)
- **Domain:** operational
- **Statement:** AI systems should propose and inform, not decide and execute. The human observer maintains decision authority. This preserves human sovereignty while leveraging AI capability. The AI proposes; the human disposes.
- **Key insight:** Capability without authorization is hazard. The human remains the check.
- **Application:** Design AI interactions where AI presents options and reasoning, human approves action.

---

## Extract Before Invent

- **H-Level:** H5 (Intelligence)
- **Domain:** operational
- **Statement:** When facing a problem, first extract relevant existing knowledge before inventing new solutions. Most problems have been solved. Search the existing knowledge base before creating new content. Novel invention should be last resort, not first instinct.
- **Key insight:** The best solution often already exists. The bottleneck is retrieval, not invention.
- **Application:** When stuck, search first. Create only when search fails.

---

## Observation Asymmetry as Power

- **H-Level:** H7 (Power)
- **Domain:** operational, strategic
- **Statement:** Power = (Your observation of reality) - (Others' observation of you). Maximize the first, minimize the second. See clearly while being seen less clearly. This is the practical application of observation primacy.
- **Key insight:** The advantage goes to whoever sees more while being seen less.
- **Application:** Improve observation capacity. Reduce attack surface. Maintain strategic illegibility where beneficial.

---

## Alignment Feels Better Than Indulgence

- **H-Level:** H10 (Meaning)
- **Domain:** operational, behavioral
- **Statement:** Actions aligned with observer values produce deeper satisfaction than actions that merely satisfy immediate desires. The feeling of coherence exceeds the feeling of gratification. This is empirically verifiable through self-observation.
- **Key insight:** What feels good short-term and what produces lasting satisfaction are different.
- **Application:** Use this asymmetry as compass. When choices conflict, lean toward alignment.

---

## Default Drift

- **H-Level:** H4 (Defense)
- **Domain:** operational
- **Statement:** The correct default is usually "do nothing" or "continue current trajectory." Action should be triggered by threshold breach, not by passage of time or desire to feel productive. Unnecessary action creates unnecessary risk.
- **Key insight:** The urge to "do something" is often noise, not signal.
- **Application:** Establish clear thresholds. Take action when thresholds breach, not before.

---

## Automate Vigilance

- **H-Level:** H4-H5 (Defense, Intelligence)
- **Domain:** operational
- **Statement:** Human attention degrades over time. Long-term vigilance fails. Build systems that alert rather than requiring constant monitoring. Automation preserves the human for decision-making rather than exhausting them on surveillance.
- **Key insight:** Vigilance that depends on sustained human attention will fail. Systems that alert preserve human capacity.
- **Application:** Replace ongoing monitoring with alert systems. Reserve human attention for decisions.
